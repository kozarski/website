I spend a lot of time wondering why things are the way they are. Sometimes it's the extra tap in an app, sometimes it's a ticket machine with seven fares I've never heard of, and sometimes it's a crossing 200 metres from where everyone actually wants to cross. I'm curious about the decisions behind these things, and how technology and thoughtful design can make everyday life a little easier and more delightful. My favourite solutions are the ones so obvious that once you see them, it's hard to imagine it ever working any other way.

Like most millennials, I grew up on the WWW, neck deep in "internet culture" and fascinated by how fast the world was (is) evolving. Remember needing an invite to Gmail? <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Gmail#Extended_beta_phase" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">I do</a>. I still love the weird, personal things people make for the web, and that spirit carries into my own little projects. I have a soft spot for the hardware of that era, too. Spinning open a Sidekick or thumb-typing on a BlackBerry remains hard to beat. I miss buttons.

I used to photograph, film (and edit) skateboarding, along with running my own skate company with friends. I'm based in London, still skating, still shooting, and still collecting more cameras than I can reasonably justify. I enjoy having room to play, whether that's using my cameras, scrubbing old footage in a timeline, or exploring motion design and 3D with <a href="https://www.maxon.net/en/cinema-4d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cinema 4D</a>, <a href="https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Unreal</a>, and <a href="https://cavalry.scenegroup.co/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cavalry</a>.

Beyond my creative interests, I've worked in software engineering, with a focus on software quality, technical troubleshooting, and endpoint management. I've worked at <a href="https://square.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Square</a>, <a href="https://www.meta.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Facebook</a> (now Meta), <a href="https://www.venmo.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Venmo</a>, <a href="https://www.apple.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Apple</a>, and <a href="https://www.stripe.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Stripe</a>.

These days, I've put some of that experience into my own iOS apps, built in Swift and SwiftUI. My current project, <a href="https://www.visitnowhere.app/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Nowhere</a>, helps you passively track, reflect on, and appreciate the places you go throughout your day, while keeping your data private and secure.

I created this blog to give everything a home: things I'm building, things I'm trying, and whatever else has taken over my brain for the week. Personal finance might turn up too. Somehow, that's also a hobby.

Want to chat about any of this? You can reach me at my <a href="mailto:coreykozarski@gmail.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">email</a>.
